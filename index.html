Why "we don't upload your files" usually means very little

Plenty of online tools promise that your files are private. Some do the work on a server and delete the file afterwards. Some do the work in your browser, but also load analytics, ads or third-party scripts that could read the page. From the outside, a visitor can't tell these apart. The promise is only as good as the company making it, and the company can change.

Nullsend is built so the promise can be checked rather than trusted:

  • The work happens on your device. Image processing, the AI model and text recognition all run in your browser with WebAssembly. There is no server that receives files, because there is no server at all: the site is a set of static files.
  • The browser enforces it. Each page carries a Content-Security-Policy with connect-src 'self'. That tells your browser to refuse any attempt to send data to another site, even from code we didn't write.
  • Nothing third-party is loaded. No analytics, no ads, no fonts or scripts from other servers. The AI models are hosted on this site and checked against published checksums when the site is built.
  • You can watch it. The meter in the header counts every byte each page sends while you use it.

What you can do here

Check a photo before you share it

Phones record where and when each photo was taken, which device took it, and sometimes much more. AI image tools may embed Content Credentials or the full prompt you used. Metadata X-ray shows all of it, explains what each item reveals, and removes it without re-compressing the image.

Cut out a subject without uploading it

The background remover runs a segmentation neural network on your own processor. It's suited to ID photos, product shots and pictures of people who never agreed to have their image sent to a third party.

Share a screenshot without leaking what's in it

Redact & Share reads the text in a screenshot, finds emails, phone numbers, card numbers, ID numbers and API keys, and covers them with solid boxes before you send it anywhere.

Questions

Is Nullsend free?

Yes. There's no account, no usage limit and no paid tier. Because the work runs on your device, serving another user costs almost nothing.

Who makes it, and how is it funded?

Nullsend is an open-source project. It has no business model that depends on your data, and the code is public so you don't have to take that on faith.

Does it work on phones?

Yes, in any modern mobile browser. The AI tools are slower on phones than on laptops, and the first run downloads their models once.
