How it works

Most background removers send your photo to a server, run a model there and send back the result. This one ships the model to you instead. The first time you use it, your browser downloads a 44 MB segmentation model from this site. After that it is cached and works offline.

The model is ISNet, a neural network built for dichotomous image segmentation: deciding, pixel by pixel, whether each pixel belongs to the main subject or to the background. It looks at a 1024 × 1024 version of your photo and produces a soft mask, which is scaled back up and applied to your original. The result has the same dimensions as the photo you started with. The model runs on your processor through ONNX Runtime compiled to WebAssembly, the same engine many desktop apps use. It uses an 8-bit quantized copy of the model, which is a quarter of the full size with very little loss in quality.

Why on-device matters for this tool

The photos people cut out are often the ones they'd least like to hand over: ID and visa photos, pictures of children, products that haven't launched yet, and people who never agreed to have their picture uploaded anywhere. Upload-based services each have their own terms about how long they keep images and what they may use them for. With an on-device tool, the question doesn't come up: the photo never leaves the tab you're looking at.

Getting the best cutout

  • One clear subject works best. The model looks for the most prominent object. With several people or items, it may keep all of them or pick one.
  • Contrast helps. A subject that stands out from its background in color or brightness gives the cleanest edge.
  • Keep the subject in focus. Motion blur and soft focus make edges ambiguous.
  • Expect trouble with see-through and very fine things. Glass, veils, smoke and single strands of hair against a busy background are hard for every segmentation model. Check the edges with the comparison slider before you use the result.
  • Use a large original. The mask is computed at 1024 pixels and then scaled, so detail in the source photo still shows in the final edges.

Choosing a background

  • Transparent: a PNG with an alpha channel, ready to place on a slide, website or design.
  • Color: fills the background with any color. Pure white (#ffffff) is what most online marketplaces and many ID photo rules ask for.
  • Blurred: keeps your original background but blurs it heavily behind the sharp subject, similar to a phone's portrait mode.

Questions

Is it free? Are there limits?

It's free, with no sign-up, watermark or limit on the number of photos. It runs on your own computer, so the only cost is a few seconds of your processor's time.

How long does it take?

The first run includes the one-time model download. After that, a typical photo takes a few seconds on a recent laptop and longer on older or low-power devices. Large photos take longer to load and save, but the model itself always works at the same size.

Does it work offline?

Yes, once you've used it once. The page and model are cached in your browser. You can also install Nullsend as an app from your browser's menu.

Can I use the images commercially?

The images you create are yours. Nullsend's source code is licensed under AGPL-3.0, which covers the software, not the pictures you make with it.

Is my photo uploaded anywhere?

No. The network meter at the top of the page counts every byte this page sends, and the page's security policy forbids connections to any other site. The only downloads are this site's own code and model.
