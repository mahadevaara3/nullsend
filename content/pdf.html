What a PDF says about you

Most people think of a PDF as a picture of a document. It's closer to a container, and the apps that make and edit PDFs write details about you and your computer into it:

  • Author and title. Word, Pages and most PDF printers copy the name from your account into the Author field. A file sent "anonymously" often names its writer.
  • Creator and Producer. The app and version that made the file, and often the operating system.
  • Created and modified dates. When the document was really written, which may not match the date printed on it.
  • XMP metadata. An XML block that can hold edit history, earlier titles, document IDs and the author again.
  • Comment authors. Every highlight and sticky note records the name of the person who added it.
  • Photos with their EXIF intact. A phone photo placed in a PDF usually keeps its camera data, including GPS coordinates of where it was taken.
  • Private app data. Illustrator, InDesign and others store their own data (PieceInfo) inside pages, sometimes including content you deleted.

When you add a PDF, the "Found in your files" box lists what that file actually contains.

What "Remove metadata" does

The new PDF is built from scratch: the pages are copied into an empty document, so its own catalog never had metadata. Then every object in it is checked. Document info, XMP blocks, private app data and comment author names are removed, and EXIF and XMP are cut out of embedded JPEG photos without re-compressing them. Finally, the file you download is read back and scanned again, and the result is shown next to the download.

Page content, text, fonts, links and form fields stay as they are. It does not remove text that is visible on the page, and it is not redaction: covering text with a black box in another app usually leaves the text underneath selectable.

How to use it

  1. Drop one or more PDFs, photos, or both. Every page shows up as a thumbnail, in the order you added them.
  2. Drag pages to reorder them. On a phone, press and hold a page first, then drag.
  3. Click pages to select them, then rotate or delete the selection.
  4. Download PDF saves all pages as one file, or only the selected ones. Split into pages saves each page as its own PDF, in a .zip. Export as images saves pages as PNG or JPEG files.

Questions

Can I open password-protected PDFs?

Not yet. Remove the password in your PDF reader first (usually by printing to a new PDF), then add the result here.

Is there a size limit?

Only your device's memory. Files of a few hundred pages work on a laptop. Very large scanned files can be slow on phones.

Will scanned text stay searchable?

Yes. Merging, splitting, rotating and cleaning keep each page's content as it was, including any text layer. Exporting as images does not keep text, because an image is only pixels.

Do photos lose quality?

Not JPEGs: they're placed in the PDF byte for byte, minus their metadata. WebP images and rotated phone photos are redrawn upright and saved as high-quality JPEGs, because PDF has no rotation flag for images.
