What a phone video says about you

Phones write metadata into every video, the same way they do for photos. Most of it is invisible in a video player:

  • GPS location. iPhones and most Android phones store the coordinates where the clip was recorded, often to within a few meters. A video filmed at home can give away your address.
  • Device make and model. For example "Apple iPhone 15 Pro", which narrows down who filmed it.
  • Software version. The exact iOS or Android version.
  • Recording date and time. When the clip was really filmed, not when it was shared.

Some apps remove this when you share, many don't, and email, cloud drives and file transfers keep everything. When you open a video here, "Found in this file" lists what it carries.

Lossless, or re-encoded

Same format, no re-encoding copies the compressed video and audio into a new file. It's fast, there's no quality loss, and it's the right choice for removing metadata or making a rough cut. A video can only be cut cleanly at a key frame, so the start may move back by up to a couple of seconds.

MP4 and WebM decode and re-encode every frame. That's slower, but it allows exact cuts, a smaller resolution and a smaller file. The work is done by your device's own video encoder through the browser's WebCodecs feature, usually the same hardware that records video, so it's much faster than encoders written in JavaScript or WebAssembly.

How to use it

  1. Drop or choose a video. It plays in the preview, and its format, size and metadata are shown.
  2. To cut it, drag the two handles, type times, or play to a point and press Set start or Set end.
  3. Choose an output. To make a file smaller, pick MP4 and a lower resolution or quality.
  4. Press Export. The file downloads, and the result shows the size change and the metadata check.

Questions

Which browsers work?

Lossless trimming and metadata removal work in any modern browser. Re-encoding needs WebCodecs: Chrome, Edge, Safari 17 and later, or Firefox 130 and later. HEVC videos from iPhones can only be re-encoded where the device can decode HEVC.

How big can the video be?

The finished file is held in memory before it downloads, so the limit is your device's memory: a few gigabytes on a laptop, less on a phone.

Does removing metadata change the picture?

No. In "Same format" mode the video and audio data are copied byte for byte. Only the descriptive tags are left out, and the rotation flag stays, so portrait videos stay upright.

Why can't I make an MP3?

Browsers don't include an MP3 encoder. M4A (AAC) sounds as good at a smaller size and plays everywhere MP3 does.
